# 📌 PART 1: EARTH AND FORMATION OF EARTH'S CRUST

## 🌍 Formation of Earth

- Earth is estimated to have **formed 4.6 billion years ago**
- Initially it was a **hot ball of fire and gas**
- Over time, the surface **cooled down gradually** and **compressed into solid rock** (exterior)
- The **interior remained in molten form**
- Earth has **4 major layers:**
  1. Crust
  2. Mantle
  3. Outer Core
  4. Inner Core

---

## 🔵 Layers of the Earth – Detailed Explanation

### 1. CRUST
- The **outermost solid shell** of Earth
- Covers the underlying **liquid mantle**
- Very thin layer — accounts for **less than 1% of Earth's volume**
- Forms an important part of the **Lithosphere**
- The **Lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates** that move constantly
- Movement of tectonic plates allows **heat to escape** from Earth's interior into space

### 2. MANTLE
- Makes up the **majority of Earth's inner structure**
- Spans approximately **2900 km**
- Contains **molten silicate**
- Tectonic plates **float on the fluidized mantle** — this keeps them in **continuous motion**

### 3. OUTER CORE
- A **fluid layer** approximately **2,400 km thick**
- Composed mainly of **iron and nickel**
- Located **above the inner core** and **below the mantle**

### 4. INNER CORE
- The **innermost part** of the Earth
- A **solid core** with a radius of approximately **1220 km**
- Exact composition is **not known** (no direct sample available)
- Temperature is estimated around **5000°C**

---

## 🌋 Formation of Earth's Crust

- 4.6 billion years ago, hot ball of fire gradually cooled → surface hardened → **land forms created**
- **Atmospheric changes** like precipitation of water **accelerated** the process
- **Low lying areas** accumulated water → formed **Seas and Oceans**
- **Landforms (tectonic plates)** floated on mantle layer
- **Dynamic movement** of tectonic plates → led to the formation of **continents** as we see today

# 📌 PART 2: LAND AS A RESOURCE

## 🌱 What is Land?

- Land is a **major constituent** of the life-supporting **Lithosphere**
- Land forms **1/5th (20%) of Earth's surface**
- Covered by: **natural forests, wetlands, grasslands, agricultural land, urban and rural settlements**
- Land is a **major source of materials** essential to humans and other organisms

---

## 🪨 Soil – Introduction

- **Soil** = the **upper weathered crust of earth** that supports plant growth
- Among all land resources, **soil is of critical importance**
- Terrestrial plants obtain **water and mineral nutrients** from soil
- Plant and animal materials **decay and release nutrients** into soil
- Soil is a **habitat for many detritus organisms**
- The study of origin, formation, and geological distribution of soil is called **Pedology**
  - Pedion = ground, logos = discourse

### Soil Formation Processes:
| Process | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Weathering** | Breaking down of rock into smaller particles |
| **Pedogenesis** | Maturation of soil through development of humus |

> 💡 Soil takes **several decades or even centuries** to form due to various physico-chemical properties involved.

# 📌 PART 3: TYPES OF LAND FORMS

## 1. 🌾 Grasslands

- One of Earth's **major biomes**
- Native vegetation of **up to 40% of Earth's terrestrial surface**
- Found on **every continent except Antarctica**
- **Ecologically and economically important**
- Provide critical **ecosystem goods and services** at local, regional, and global scales
- Occur under a **broad range of climatic conditions** — water is generally limiting
- Many grasslands experience **periodic droughts** and a dormant season
- Sensitive to **climate variability and climate change**

---

## 2. 💧 Wetlands

- A **distinct ecosystem** that is **flooded by water** — either permanently or seasonally
- **Oxygen-free processes prevail** in wetlands
- Primary distinguishing factor: **characteristic vegetation of aquatic plants** adapted to unique **hydric soil**

### Functions of Wetlands:
- Water **purification**
- Water **storage**
- Processing of **carbon and other nutrients**
- **Stabilization of shorelines**
- Support of **plants and animals**
- Considered the **most biologically diverse** of all ecosystems
- Home to a **wide range of plant and animal life**

---

## 3. 🌲 Forests

- A **complex ecosystem** predominantly composed of **trees, shrubs** with a **closed canopy**
- Storehouses of large variety of **life forms** — plants, mammals, birds, insects, reptiles
- Contain **abundant microorganisms and fungi** that decompose dead organic matter → **enriching soil**
- Nearly **4 billion hectares** of forest cover Earth's surface
- Roughly **30% of Earth's total land area** is forested

---

## 4. 🌾 Agricultural Land

- Land **devoted to agriculture** — systematic and controlled use for:
  - **Rearing livestock**
  - **Production of crops** to produce food for humans
- Synonymous with **farmland, cropland, pasture, or rangeland**

---

## 5. ❄️ Tundra

- A biome where **tree growth is hindered** by low temperatures and short growing seasons
- Vegetation: **dwarf shrubs, sedges, grasses, mosses, lichens**
- Tundra soil is **rich in nitrogen and phosphorus**
- Three types:
  1. **Arctic tundra**
  2. **Alpine tundra**
  3. **Antarctic tundra**

---

## 6. 🏜️ Deserts

- A **barren area** of landscape where **little precipitation occurs**
- Living conditions are **hostile** for plant and animal life
- Can be classified by:
  - Amount of **precipitation**
  - **Temperature**
  - **Causes of desertification**
  - **Geographical location**
- Formed by **weathering processes** — large temperature variation between day and night puts strains on rocks → rocks break into pieces
- Although rain seldom occurs, occasional **downpours can cause flash floods**

---

## 7. 🏙️ Urban Establishment

- Land used for **buildings, roads, infrastructure** for human settlements
- Represents **human-modified environment**

---

## Other Landforms (from diagram):
Mountain Range, Glacier, Volcano, Plateau, Canyon, Valley, Lake, River, Delta, Basin, Desert, Inlet, Strait, Peninsula, Archipelago, Island

# 📌 PART 4: CONSERVATION OF LAND FORMS

## Definition:
Landform conservation is the **protection and wise use of the land base** including its form, soil, and associated biophysical processes.

It is an approach that encourages **planning, design, and construction practices** which:

1. **Minimize disruption** to natural form and related ecological processes
2. **Enhance protection** of biophysical features in a natural state
3. Keep a greater portion of a site in an **open-space character**
4. **Protect endangered flora and fauna** from extinction
5. **Preserve traditional practices** associated with these areas (of great cultural importance)
6. Prevent **drastic effects on climate** — changes to landform can cause global climatic concerns

# 📌 PART 5: DEFORESTATION

## Definition:
Deforestation is the **conversion of forested areas to non-forest land use** such as:
- Arable land
- Urban use
- Logged area
- Wasteland

### FAO (Food and Agriculture Organisation of UNO) Definition:
> "The conversion of forest to another land use **OR** the long-term reduction of tree canopy cover **below the 10% threshold**"

---

## Important Points about Deforestation:

- Can be **deliberate** (for agriculture or urban development)
- Can be **unintentional** (consequence of uncontrolled grazing — prevents natural regeneration of young trees)
- Combined effect of **grazing + fires** = major cause of deforestation in dry areas
- Implies **long-term (>10 years) or permanent loss** of forest cover
- It is **not just conversion** of forest to non-forest but also **degradation** that reduces:
  - Forest quality
  - Density and structure of trees
  - Ecological services
  - Biomass of plants and animals
  - Species diversity
  - Genetic diversity

---

## 🌿 Related Concepts:

### Forest Degradation:
- A process leading to **'temporary or permanent deterioration in density or structure of vegetation cover or species composition'**
- It is a change in forest attributes that leads to **lower productive capacity** caused by increased disturbances
- Different from deforestation but often used interchangeably

### Forest Fragmentation:
- Any process that results in the **conversion of formerly continuous forest into patches** of forest separated by non-forested lands
- A **complex phenomenon** resulting from dynamic interactions between the natural landscape and society's ever-increasing land demands
- Creates a **mosaic of natural and human-modified environments**

---

## Agents vs Causes of Deforestation:

| Agents | Causes |
|---|---|
| Those who **clear the forest** (individuals, corporations, government agencies, development projects) | **Processes** that lead to deforestation |

### Table: Important Agents leading to Deforestation, Degradation, and Fragmentation

| Agent | Link to Deforestation |
|---|---|
| Slash-and-burn farmers | Clear forest to grow subsistence and cash crops |
| Commercial farmers | Clear forest to plant commercial cash crops |
| Cattle ranchers | Clear forest to plant pasture |
| Livestock herders | Intensification of herding activities |
| Loggers | Remove commercial timber; roads provide access to other users |
| Commercial tree planters | Clear forest fallow to establish plantations |
| Firewood collectors | Intensification can lead to deforestation |
| Mining & petroleum industries | Roads and seismic lines provide access; localized deforestation |
| Land settlement planners | Relocation of people into forested areas |
| Infrastructure developers | Road/highway construction through forests; flooding by dams |

---

## Causes of Deforestation

Deforestation is attributed to **socio-demographic factors** such as population growth and political economy.

### 1. Direct Causes of Deforestation:
- **Logging** — loggers build roads into primary forests, remove trees; logging destroys **45–74% of residual trees**
- Conversion of forested lands for **agriculture**
- **Cattle-raising**
- **Urbanization**
- **Mining and oil exploitation**
- **Acid rain**
- **Forest fire**

> Historical Note: Countries like **Ireland and Scotland** were once almost entirely forested but were nearly completely cleared under British rule to provide timber for English shipbuilders.

### 2. Underlying Causes of Deforestation:
- Unpriced forest goods and services
- Monopolies in Market
- Wrong incentives
- Improper regulatory measures
- Illegal activities in forest land
- Weak ownership of the government
- Population growth
- Economic growth
- Excessive consumption
- Global Warming
- War

---

## ✅ Afforestation:

- **Conversion from other land uses into forest**, or increase of canopy cover **above the 10% threshold**
- **Reverse of deforestation**
- Includes areas actively converted through **silvicultural measures**
- Also includes **natural transitions** into forest (e.g., abandoned agricultural land, burnt-over areas)

## ✅ Reforestation:

- **Re-establishment of forests** after deforestation **due to natural sources**

# 📌 PART 6: EFFECT OF LAND USE CHANGES

## What is Land Use?

- **'Land use'** refers specifically to the **purposes for which land is occupied or managed** for human ends
- Common categories: **cropland, pasture, settlements**
- Land **not used by humans** = designated as **'wild' or 'natural'** land
- Land **lightly used** by humans = designated as **'semi-natural'**

---

## Direct vs Indirect Land Use:

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Land Use** | A specific piece of land and how it is being used at a specific moment | Cattle rearing in pasture land — cows grazing directly |
| **Indirect Land Use** | The 'use' happens later in the value chain when a product is consumed or used | Cattle rearing in cropland — conversion of wild land into fodder cropland |

---

## Effects of Land Use Changes

Land use changes occur **constantly and at many scales** and can have **specific and cumulative effects** on:
- Air and water quality
- Watershed function
- Generation of waste
- Extent and quality of wildlife habitat
- Climate
- Human health

### a. Effects of Land Development:

Land development creates **impervious surfaces** through roads, parking lots, and other structures.

**Impacts of Impervious Surfaces:**
- Contribute to **nonpoint source water pollution** by limiting soil's capacity to filter runoff
- Affect **peak flow and water volume** → heighten erosion potential
- **Increase storm water runoff** → delivers more pollutants to water bodies (dirt, oils, nutrients from fertilizers, toxic compounds)
- **Affect groundwater aquifer recharge**

**Point Source Discharges:**
- Industrial and municipal wastewater facilities → contribute **toxic compounds and heated water**

**Suburbanization Effects:**
- **Increased air pollution** from vehicle use → higher air pollutant concentrations → exacerbates health problems like **asthma**
- Formation of **"heat islands"** — domes of warmer air over urban areas caused by:
  - Loss of trees and shrubs
  - Absorption of heat by pavement and buildings
- Heat islands affect **local, regional, and global climate** + air quality

---

### b. Effects of Agricultural Land Use:

Agricultural land uses affect the **quality of water and watersheds:**

- Types of crops planted, tillage practices, and irrigation can **limit water availability** for other uses
- **Livestock grazing in riparian zones** → reduces stream bank vegetation → increases water temperatures, sedimentation, and nutrient levels
- **Runoff from pesticides, fertilizers, animal manure** → degrades water quality
- May result in **loss of native habitats** or increased **wind erosion and dust** → exposes humans to particulate matter and chemicals
- Some land uses **accelerate spread of invasive species**
- Agricultural practices (overgrazing, land conversion, fertilization, use of chemicals) → enhance growth of **invasive plants** → alters fish and wildlife habitat → reduces biodiversity → creates health risks

---

## 📖 Case Study: Shire River in Malawi, Africa

- The **Shire River in southern Africa** is a prime example of where **land use land cover change (LUCC)** has caused severe soil erosion and heavy siltation downstream
- The river is important for: agriculture, domestic purposes, electricity generation
- **Nkula B Hydroelectric Power Station** is situated across the Shire River
- The dam has been threatened by **massive siltation** due to increased human population and agricultural activities

### Key Findings:
- **Strong link exists between land use change and soil erosion**
- Good vegetation cover → **promotes infiltration** and soil retention
- **Deforestation** → increased runoff → stronger soil erosion
- Erosion of soil under continuous cultivation = **most serious form of resource degradation in Malawi**
- Rate of soil loss: currently **29 t/ha/year** (higher than previous estimate of 20 t/ha/year)

# 📌 PART 7: SOIL

## Definition of Soil:

**Soil Science Society of America (SSSA)** defines soil as:
> "The unconsolidated mineral or organic material on the immediate surface of the earth that serves as a **natural medium for the growth of land plants.**"

### Key Points:
- Soil is composed of: **inorganic particles, organic matter, air, water, and a variety of organisms**
- Takes **decades or even centuries** to develop soil horizons
- Human activities create worldwide problems like **soil erosion** and **depletion of fertility**
- The **yield of all biotic products** in terrestrial ecosystems depends on **soil fertility**

# 📌 PART 9: ECOLOGICAL AND ECONOMIC IMPORTANCE OF SOIL

- Almost **35% of all greenhouse gases (GHGs)** released due to anthropogenic activities since 1850 are linked to **land use changes**
- **Soil stores CO₂** and other GHGs in soil organic matter → carbon sequestration
- Crop, grazing, forest lands, and wetlands all have potential to **mitigate GHG emissions** through proper management

### Benefits of Soil Organic Matter:
- Filters and **cleans water**
- Enhances **water retention and storage**
- **Mitigates impacts of extreme weather** events
- Improves **soil structure**
- **Reduces soil erosion**
- Provides **microbial habitats**
- Source of **long-term, slow-release nutrients**

# 📌 PART 10: SOIL DEGRADATION / LAND DEGRADATION AND ITS IMPACT

## Causes of Soil/Land Degradation:

1. Soil Erosion
2. Loss of Soil Fertility
3. Landslide
4. Soil sealing through urbanisation and industrialisation
5. Soil contamination
6. Salination

---

### 1. 🌊 Soil Erosion

- **Movement of water and air removes topsoil** from the land
- Abundant plant cover **significantly reduces** soil erosion
- **Human activities accelerate** soil erosion by removing natural plant cover
- From croplands in India, **millions of tons of topsoil** are eroded into sea each year
- Erosion causes **significant loss of soil fertility** — transports organic matter and nutrients
- Eroded soil enters **streams, rivers, lakes as sediments** → affects water quality and habitats of aquatic organisms

---

### 2. 🌾 Depletion of Soil Fertility

- When natural vegetation is removed to develop agricultural systems:
  - **Nutrients stored in vegetation are removed**
  - **Organic matter and nutrients accumulated in soil are also lost**
- From agricultural systems, **nutrients are exported through crop harvest**
- Over time, **agricultural soil inevitably loses its fertility**

---

### 3. 🏔️ Landslide Damage

- Landslide refers to all kinds of **rapid mass movements** involving:
  - Rapid flow
  - Slip
  - Falling, sliding, or flowing (or combination)
- Since landslide involves movement of **soil and vegetation mass**:
  - Self-sustaining **soil-plant system is fragmented**
  - Leads to **disruption of community structure**
  - Disrupts **nutrient cycling**
  - **Loss of soil with nutrients** from ecosystem
  - **Destruction of habitat** → loss of biodiversity
  - **Decrease in forest biomass and soil fertility**
- Recovery depends on **intensity of disturbance**:
  - Low intensity disturbance → **faster recovery**
  - Long-term disturbance → **delayed recovery**

---

### 4. 🏙️ Soil Sealing through Urbanisation and Industrialisation

- **Urbanisation** = Establishment of infrastructure for modern life (housing, roads, land developments)
- **Soil Sealing** = Closure of soil pores on the surface due to materials used for urbanisation

**Effects of Soil Sealing:**
- Soil **unable to absorb rainwater** for infiltration
- Cannot perform **general filtering** functions
- **Changes water flow patterns** in surrounding soils
- Soil sealing is **almost irreversible**
- Increasing concern among governments and environmental regulators about this **permanent loss of soil** and loss of **ecosystem functions**

---

### 5. ☠️ Soil Contamination

- **Introduction of contaminants into soil** → damage or loss of soil functions
- Can lead to **contamination of water**
- Contaminants above certain levels → multiple **negative consequences** for:
  - The **food chain**
  - **Human health**
  - All types of **ecosystems**
  - Other **natural resources**

---

### 6. 🧂 Salination

- **Accumulation of soluble salts** (principally sodium, magnesium, and calcium) in soils
- To the extent that **crop production is severely reduced**
- Often associated with **insufficient irrigation practices**
  - Irrigation water contains variable amounts of salts
  - In regions with **low rainfall** and **high evapotranspiration rates**, salts build up in the soil surface layer
  - Salts cannot be washed out → **buildup in soil surface layer**

---

# 📌 PART 11: SOIL CONSERVATION

## History:

- **1907** = Important year in soil conservation history
- **United States Department of Agriculture** declared an official policy of **land protection**
- **Iceland** established the **world's first soil conservation service** in the same year

---

## Strategies to Conserve Soil:

### i. Reduce Soil Disturbance

- **Intensive soil tillage** is one of the greatest catalysts for soil degradation
- Plough tillage systems = **most detrimental practice** to soil health (long and short term)
- Breaking up soil → many problems
- Reduced tillage methods:
  - **Strip tillage**
  - **No tillage**
- These can produce **similar crop yields** as conventional plow-till methods in arid and semi-arid farmlands

---

### ii. Crop Rotation Practices

- Farmers grow crops from **different plant families** in a well-planned sequence over **multiple seasons** in the same field
- Can be of **any length** depending on farmer's preferences or local regulations
- Key factor: **variety of crops from different families** grown in same soil
- **Benefits:**
  - Breaks the **disease cycle** in a given field
  - Greater crop diversity → greater **diversity of soil microbiomes** → better soil health

---

### iii. Cover Cropping Practices

- Cover crops are plants grown **in between cash crop cycles** to:
  - **Protect land from erosion**
  - Add **root exudates and biomass** for soil improvement
- Cover crop biomass **must be returned to soil** after the growing period for full benefit
- **Legumes** fix **nitrogen in soil** through association with bacteria called **Rhizobium**
  - Rhizobium converts **atmospheric nitrogen to plant-useable nitrogen**
  - When leguminous cover crops are incorporated into soil → nitrogen becomes available for the **next crop**
  - Reduces the **external nitrogen demand** of the following cash crop

---

### iv. Diversify Production Systems

- Diversified farming includes:
  - Mixed varieties and **mixed cropping**
  - **Integrating livestock** into crop production
  - **Cover cropping**
  - **Crop rotation**
  - **Fallowing fields**
  - **Hedgerows and buffer strips**
- Benefits:
  - Improvements in **nutrient cycling**
  - Better **soil water management**
  - Better **pest control**
  - Habitats for **pollinators**

---

### v. Add Soil Organic Amendments

- Organic amendments = any material of **plant or animal origin** added to soil to:
  - **Improve soil conditions**
  - **Stimulate biodiversity**
- Examples: **manure, compost, biochar**, and similar materials
- Regular addition of organic amendments → improves:
  - **Soil fertility**
  - **Soil biological functions**
  - **Soil physical characteristics**

---

### vi. Integrate Livestock into Cropping Systems

- Integrating livestock through **winter grazing** benefits producers:
  - Increases **efficiency of farm resource utilization**
  - Decreases need for **synthetic fertilizers and pesticides**
  - Improves **soil health**
- Supports growth of **diverse microbial communities** → promotes soil organic matter accumulation
- **Grazing speeds up decomposition** of leftover plant residues → enhances availability of nutrients like **nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur**
- **Hoof action** of animals → brings more residue in contact with soil
- **Saliva of grazing animals** → helps growth and release of nutrients

---

### vii. Terrace Farming

- Practice of **cutting flat areas out of hilly or mountainous landscape** to grow crops
- Practiced from **rice fields of Asia** to **steep slopes of the Andes in South America**
- **Benefits:**
  - Helps **prevent erosion and soil runoff**
  - Prevents what would be an immediate result of tilling a hillside without terraces
  - By using terraces, a hillside can remain **productive for a long time** as long as soil is properly cared for

---

### viii. Shelter Belts

- In **coastal and dry regions**, rows of trees are planted
- Purpose: **check wind movement** to protect soil cover
- Acts as a natural **wind barrier** to prevent wind erosion

---

# 📌 PART 12: IMPACT OF SOIL DEGRADATION ON AGRICULTURE AND FOOD SECURITY

## Key Points:

- The gravity of soil degradation depends on the **type of degradation process**
- **Soil erosion and salinification** are very serious — can drive farmers to:
  - **Abandon the land**, OR
  - Face **very high management costs**

## Global Perspective – India:

- India supports:
  - **18% of world's human population**
  - **15% of world's livestock population**
  - But has only **2.4% of world's land area**
- Soil degradation is causing:
  - **Decline in crop productivity**
  - **Huge economic loss**
  - **Threat to food security** and livelihoods of farmers

## Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA):

- **Nutrient depletion** = primary form of soil degradation in SSA
- Leading to **decline in crop productivity**
- Linked to **hunger and poverty**

## Additional Impact:

- Soil degradation forces farmers to look for **new land**
- Most new land = **marginal land** or **tropical forests** (especially in Latin America and Africa)
- Such soils are **not suitable for agricultural production**
- Require **high investments** to become productive
- Overall effects of soil degradation → **major threat to food security**, especially in poor regions

# 📌 PART 13: SUSTAINABLE LAND USE PLANNING

## Definition:

> "The **systematic assessment** of land and water potential, alternatives for land use, and economic and social conditions in order to **select and adopt the best land use options**."

**Purpose:** Select and put into practice those land uses that will:
- **Best meet the needs of people**
- While **safeguarding resources for the future**

---

## Historical Evolution of the Concept of Land:

| Time Period | Perception of Land |
|---|---|
| 1700s | Land = **Wealth** |
| Later | Land = **Commodity** |
| Further Later | Land = **Scarce Resource** |
| From 1980s onwards | Land = **Scarce Community Resource** (representing both commodity and wealth) |

---

## Sustainable Land Use – Definition:

> "The use of components of land in a way, and at a rate, that **does not lead to the long-term decline of land**, thereby maintaining its potential to **meet the needs and aspirations of present and future generations.**"

**Sustainable Land Management (SLM)** encompasses the:
- **Ecological** dimension
- **Economic** dimension
- **Socio-cultural** dimension of sustainable development

---

## Sustainable Land Use Helps In:

1. **Protecting agricultural land** from urban and peri-urban encroachment
2. **Protecting natural capital** from urban and peri-urban encroachment
3. **Preventing or limiting exposure** of saline and acid sulphate soils
4. **Rehabilitation and/or avoidance** of contaminated sites
5. **Adaptation** to salinization and rising groundwater levels
6. Ensuring land use **reflects land capability**
7. **Protection of groundwater quality** and quantity
8. **Protection of water quality** and minimization of erosion through water-sensitive urban design
9. **Minimizing eutrophication** and other pollution of surface and groundwater
10. **Establishing appropriate buffers** between development, coastal estuaries, and water foreshores
11. **Floodplain management**
12. **Preventing or limiting vegetation clearing**
13. **Protecting natural habitats** from destruction and fragmentation
14. **Preservation and enhancement** of ecological corridors
15. **Reducing car dependence** by transport demand management
16. **Accounting for sea-level rise** and increased storm surge from coastal development
