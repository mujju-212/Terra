# 📌 PART 1: INTRODUCTION TO WATER

## 💧 Water – The Cradle of Life

- Water is an **important resource** available to all living organisms
- It is called the **"cradle of life"** on Earth
- Life **started on Earth in water** and is surviving due to the presence of water
- Water exists in the form of **hydrological cycle** — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, surface runoff, underground flow, etc.

---

## 🌊 Two Main Sources of Water:

| Source | Description |
|---|---|
| **Surface Water** | Found in lakes, rivers, and reservoirs |
| **Groundwater** | Lies under the surface of the land, travels through and fills openings in rocks |

### Key Terms:
- **Aquifer** = Rocks that store and transmit groundwater (made up of gravel, sand, sandstone, or limestone)
- Groundwater must be **pumped from an aquifer** to the Earth's surface for use

---

# 📌 PART 2: SOURCES OF WATER

## Four Main Sources of Water:

### 1. 🌧️ Rain Water

**a. From roofs of houses and dwellings:**
- Collected and stored in **underground tanks or cisterns**
- Used for **individual supply**

**b. From Prepared Catchments:**
- Given a **suitable lining and slope**
- Collected in **small reservoirs**
- Used for **community supply**

---

### 2. 🏞️ Surface Water

**a. From rivers by continuous draft:**
- Without any diversion works
- Direct extraction from flowing rivers

**b. From river diversions:**
- Water routed through **canal into treatment plant**

**c. From reservoir storage:**
- Water stored in large dams/reservoirs and then distributed

**d. From direct intake from natural lakes:**
- Direct withdrawal from existing natural lakes

---

### 3. 🪨 Ground Water

**a. From natural springs:**
- Water naturally emerging from ground

**b. From wells and bore wells:**
- Dug wells and deep bore wells for groundwater extraction

**c. From infiltration galleries:**
- Horizontal tunnels/galleries dug near rivers to collect percolated water

**d. From river side radial collector wells:**
- Special wells with horizontal arms radiating outward to collect groundwater near rivers

---

### 4. ♻️ Water Obtained from Reclamation

**a. Desalination:**
- Removal of salts from seawater to make it usable
- Used in coastal and water-scarce areas

**b. Re-use of treated waste water:**
- Treated sewage or industrial wastewater is reused
- Important for water conservation

# 📌 PART 4: RIVERS IN INDIA – INDIAN WATER RESOURCES

## Overview:

- India is **blessed with many rivers**
- **Land slope** determines the river to which rainwater falling on an area will eventually flow
- **River basin / Catchment area** = The area from which rain will flow into a particular river
- **Shape and size of river basin** is determined by topography

---

## Major River Basin Groups in India:

### 1. 🔵 Indus System
- Comprises: **River Indus** and its tributaries
- Tributaries: **Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej**
- Origin: **North** — flow in **West or South-West** direction
- Eventually flow into **Arabian Sea through Pakistan**

---

### 2. 🟢 Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna System
- Main rivers: **Ganga, Brahmaputra, Meghna** and their tributaries
- Ganga tributaries: **Yamuna, Sone, Gandak, Kosi**, and many others
- All eventually flow into **Bay of Bengal through Bangladesh**
- Note: Some tributaries are **larger than independent rivers**
  - Example: **Yamuna** (tributary of Ganga) has a larger catchment area than **Tapi** (a small peninsular river)

---

### 3. 🟡 Rivers of Rajasthan and Gujarat
- Rivers: **Mahi, Sabarmati, Luni**, etc.
- Rivers of **arid regions** — carry relatively **little flow**
- Some flow to **Arabian Sea through Gujarat**
- Some are **land-locked** — flow lost through **percolation and evaporation** in vast arid regions

---

### 4. 🟠 East Flowing Peninsular Rivers
- Important members: **Damodar, Mahanadi, Brahmani, Baitrani, Subarnarekha, Krishna-Godavari, and Kaveri**
- All flow into **Bay of Bengal** at various places along **Eastern coast of India**

---

### 5. 🔴 West Flowing Peninsular Rivers
- Rivers: **Narmada and Tapi**
- Originate in **Central India**
- Flow in **Western direction** to meet **Arabian Sea** south of Gujarat

---

### 6. 🟣 Western Coast Rivers
- Located in: **Coastal Maharashtra, Karnataka, and entire Kerala**
- Rivers are **small in length** but carry **significant amount of water**
- Due to **very high rainfall in Western Ghats**
- They drain only **3% of India's land area** but carry **11% of India's water resources**

> 💡 This shows the **mismatch between land area and water availability** in different regions of India.

# 📌 PART 5: USES OF WATER

## Types of Water Use:

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Productive Use** | Water used for irrigation of food crops, fodder crops, medicinal herbs |
| **Consumptive Use** | Water consumed for domestic purposes — drinking, cooking, washing |
| **Commercial Use** | Water used for commercial and recreational activities |

---

## Water Use Sectors (Detailed):

### 1. 🌾 Agricultural Use
- **69% of worldwide water use** is for irrigation
- In some areas, irrigation is **necessary** to grow any crop at all
- In other areas, it permits **more profitable crops** or **enhances crop yield**
- Single largest user of water globally

---

### 2. 🏭 Industrial Use
- **15% of worldwide water use** is industrial
- Major industrial users:
  - **Power plants** — water for cooling or as a power source (hydroelectric plants)
  - **Ore and oil refineries** — water in chemical processes
  - **Manufacturing plants** — water as a solvent
- Industrial water consumption is **generally lower** than agricultural use

---

### 3. 🏠 Household / Domestic Use
- **15% of worldwide water use** is for household purposes
- Uses include: **drinking water, bathing, cooking, sanitation, gardening**
- Basic household water requirement: **approximately 50 litres per person per day** (excluding water for gardens)

---

### 4. 🎣 Recreational Use
- Usually a **very small but growing** percentage of total water use
- Mostly tied to **reservoirs**
- Examples: **anglers, water skiers, nature enthusiasts, swimmers**
- If a reservoir is kept fuller than otherwise for recreation → water retained is categorized as recreational usage

---

## Framework for Water Allocation:
- The framework for allocating water resources to water users is known as **"Water Rights"**

# 📌 PART 7: INTER BASIN WATER TRANSFER (IBWT)

## Concept and Need:

- **Inter Basin Water Transfer (IBWT)** = Transfer of water from a **surplus river basin** to a **deficit river basin**
- Concept is to **explore and implement** IBWT to:
  - Ensure **uniform distribution** of available natural water resources
  - **Mitigate consequences** of hydrologic extremes (floods and droughts)

---

## Why IBWT is Essential for India:

- India's population is **growing at an alarming rate** → IBWT is the need of the hour
- Helps **prevent formation of deltas** along the coastline
- Prevents **migration of people** from drought-affected regions
- India's **monsoons are highly orographic** → causing:
  - **Floods in North and North-Eastern regions**
  - **Droughts in other areas**
- Government spends **huge amounts annually** on flood and drought relief → better to invest in IBWT for long-term benefits
- IBWT is the **only solution** to maintain regional balance in sectors like:
  - Agriculture
  - Industry
  - Power generation
  - Domestic supply
  - Navigation

# 📌 PART 9: GROUNDWATER

## Definition and Explanation:

- **Groundwater** = Water that seeps through rocks and soil and is **stored below the ground**
- Rocks in which groundwater is stored are called **Aquifers**
- Aquifers are typically made up of: **Gravel, Sand, Sandstone, or Limestone**
- Water moves through these rocks because they have **large connected spaces** that make them **permeable**

---

## Key Terms in Groundwater:

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Aquifer** | Rock that stores and transmits groundwater |
| **Saturated Zone** | The area where water fills the aquifer completely |
| **Water Table** | The depth from the surface at which groundwater is found |

### Water Table Facts:
- Can be as **shallow as one foot** below ground
- Can be **a few hundred meters deep**
- **Heavy rains** → Water table **rises**
- **Continuous extraction** of groundwater → Water table **falls**

---

# 📌 PART 10: GROUNDWATER POTENTIAL IN INDIA

## Four Major Groundwater Regions:

### 1. 🪨 Hard-Rock Aquifers of Peninsular India

- Represent around **65% of India's overall aquifer surface area**
- Found in **central peninsular India** — land underlain by hard-rock formations
- Give rise to a **complex and extensive low-storage aquifer system**
- **Water level drops rapidly** once the water table falls by more than **2–6 meters**
- Located **south of Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains**
- Rock types: **Consolidated sedimentary rocks, Deccan Trap basalts, crystalline rocks**
- States: **Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Kerala**
- Movement of groundwater **restricted to weathered residuum and interconnected fractures** at deeper levels
- **Limited groundwater potential** in this region
- Rocks weathered to a depth of **30m** under tropical conditions
- Groundwater found within depth of **less than 50m** (occasionally down to 100m, rarely beyond)
- Groundwater development is largely through **dug wells**
- **Valley fills** are often dependable sources of water supply
- Yield of wells in hard rocks: **2–10 lps** (litres per second)

---

### 2. 🌊 Alluvial Aquifers of the Indo-Gangetic Plains

- Found in **Gangetic and Indus plains** in Northern India
- Have **significant storage spaces** → valuable source of fresh water supply
- However, due to **excessive groundwater extraction** and **low recharge rates** → risk of **irreversible overexploitation**
- Region covers about **850,000 sq km**
- States covered: **Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Assam, West Bengal**
- Accounts for **more than 1/4th of India's land area**
- Underlain by **thick piles of sediments** of Tertiary and Quaternary age
- **Alluvial fill exceeds 1000 m at places** → most potential and productive groundwater reservoir in the country
- Characterized by **regionally extensive and highly productive multi-aquifer systems**
- Groundwater development is **still sub-optimal** in most states except **Haryana and Punjab**
- Deeper wells in Indo-Gangetic plains have yield ranging from **25–50 lps**

---

### 3. 🏔️ Northern Mountainous Terrain and Hilly Areas

- Highly rugged mountainous terrain in **Himalayan region**
- Extends from **Kashmir to Arunachal Pradesh**
- Characterized by **steep slopes and high runoff**
- Underlain by rocks: **Granites, slate, sandstone, and limestone** (age from Paleozoic to Cenozoic)
- **Yield potential: 1 to 40 lps**
- Offers **very little scope for groundwater storage**
- However, acts as **major source of recharge** for the vast **Indo-Gangetic and Brahmaputra alluvial plains**

---

### 4. 🏖️ Coastal Areas

- Coastal areas have **thick cover of alluvial deposits** of Pleistocene to Recent age
- Form **potential multi-aquifer systems** in: **Gujarat, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa**
- However, **inherent quality problems** and **risk of seawater ingress** impose severe constraints in development
- **Groundwater overdevelopment** in these areas → **risk of saline water ingress**
- Groundwater prospects vary from **5–25 lps** depending on local conditions

---

## Groundwater Availability in India (Statistics):

- As of **April 2015**:
  - **Annual water availability** in terms of natural runoff in rivers: **1,869 Billion Cubic Meters (BCM)/year**
  - **Usable water resources**: **1,123 BCM/year**
  - The difference is due to **constraints of topography** and **uneven distribution** in various river basins
  - Entire 1,869 BCM/year **cannot be extracted** due to these constraints

# 📌 PART 15: RECHARGE OF GROUNDWATER

## Natural Recharge:

- Flow below the land surface takes place due to the process of **infiltration**
- **Recharge of aquifers** takes place during precipitation → water infiltrates into the **pores in the soil strata**
- Soil will **not get completely saturated** unless water supply is maintained for **prolonged periods**
- If water is applied **only intermittently** → there may be **no recharge** during the first infiltration or even between two subsequent infiltrations

---

## Artificial Recharge of Groundwater:

### Why Artificial Recharge is Needed:
- **Natural replenishment** of groundwater reservoir is a **slow process**
- Often **unable to keep pace** with excessive and continued exploitation
- This results in **declining groundwater levels** and **depletion of groundwater resources**

### Definition:
> "Artificial recharge aims at **augmenting the natural replenishment** of groundwater storage by some method of construction, spreading of water, or by **artificially changing natural conditions.**"

### Uses of Artificial Recharge:
- **Reducing overdraft** of groundwater
- **Conserving surface run-off**
- **Increasing available groundwater supplies**

### Types of Artificial Recharge:
- **Incidental Recharge** = by-product of normal water utilization activities
- **Deliberate Recharge** = planned specifically to recharge groundwater

### Methods of Artificial Recharge:

**Planned methods:**
- Storing water in **pits, tanks** etc. to feed the aquifer

**Unplanned/Incidental methods:**
- Applied irrigation
- Leakages from pipes

---

### Artificial Recharge Techniques:

#### A. Direct Techniques:

**i. Surface (Spreading) Methods:**
1. **Flooding** — spreading water over large areas of land
2. **Basins or Percolation Tank** — water stored in basins/tanks to percolate into ground
3. **Stream Augmentation** — adding water to streams to increase seepage
4. **Ditch and Furrow System** — shallow ditches and furrows to spread water

**ii. Sub-surface Methods:**
1. **Recharge Well** — injecting water directly into aquifer through wells
2. **Recharge Pit/Shaft** — pits dug to allow water to seep into the ground
3. **Dug Well** — existing wells used for recharge

#### B. Indirect Techniques:
- **Induced Recharge** — placing wells near rivers/streams to induce seepage from the surface water body into the aquifer

# 📌 PART 16: SEAWATER INGRESS IN COASTAL AQUIFERS

## Background:

- Unconsolidated deltaic and coastal sediments form **thick and regionally extensive aquifers**
- Have **prolific yield potential** that can sustain deep, moderate to high capacity tube wells
- Considerable **fresh groundwater resources** have been identified along the east coast
- However, **inherent quality problems** restrict their development

---

## What is Seawater Ingress (Intrusion)?

- Groundwater in coastal aquifers exists in a **fragile dynamic equilibrium with seawater**
- **Indiscriminate exploitation** of groundwater from coastal aquifers → **disturbs this equilibrium**
- Results in development of **landward hydraulic gradient**
- This ultimately leads to **seawater intrusion into fresh water aquifers**

### States already suffering from Salinity Ingress:
- **Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh**

---

## Measures to Prevent / Control Seawater Intrusion:

| Measure | Explanation |
|---|---|
| i. **Regulation of groundwater development** in coastal areas | Controlled extraction to avoid over-pumping |
| ii. **Formation of a freshwater ridge** parallel to the coast through artificial recharge | Creating a barrier of fresh water to stop saltwater ingress |
| iii. **Formation of a pumping trough** through pumping wells aligned parallel to coast | A line of wells pumping out seawater intrusion zone |
| iv. **Reduce water use** | Conserving water reduces demand and hence less extraction |
| v. **Reduce pump depth** | Shallow pumps extract less saline water compared to deep pumps |
| vi. **Pump less water more frequently** | Small and frequent extractions are better than large volumes at once |
| vii. **Well location to be at least 50m from the coast** | Distance from coast reduces risk of seawater mixing |
| viii. **Avoid deep wells close to the coast** | Deep coastal wells are most vulnerable to saltwater intrusion |
